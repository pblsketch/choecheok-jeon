A wide hand-painted antique sea chart of East Asia for the world map screen of a story game, seen straight from above like a flat map. Absolutely no text, no letters, no characters, no numbers, no place names, no calligraphy, no labels, no seals, no stamps, no signatures, no watermarks, no compass letters, no grid lines, no borders drawn around countries, no frame.

Geography must be accurate, like a real map with north at the top, covering roughly longitude 98E to 150E and latitude 8N to 43N:
- The LEFT third of the picture is the Chinese mainland (warm ochre land with soft green hills, small mountain ranges and a few winding rivers). Its eastern coastline runs from the top-left area down toward the lower-left, bulging east at the middle (the Yangtze river mouth and the Hangzhou bay at about 42 percent from the left and 36 percent from the top), then curving southwest along the south China coast.
- In the BOTTOM-LEFT corner is the Indochina peninsula (Vietnam): a long thin S-shaped coast running from about 22 percent from the left at 62 percent from the top down to the bottom edge at about 15 percent from the left, with the South China Sea to its east. A medium island (Hainan) sits just off the coast at about 23 percent from the left and 68 percent from the top.
- A medium elongated island (Taiwan) lies off the China coast at about 44 percent from the left and 55 percent from the top.
- The Shandong peninsula pokes east from the China coast at about 43 percent from the left and 14 percent from the top, with the Yellow Sea to its east.
- The Korean peninsula hangs down from the TOP edge at about 55 to 64 percent from the left, its southern tip with many small islands reaching about 24 percent from the top.
- The Japanese islands form a long curved chain on the RIGHT: Kyushu at about 61 to 65 percent from the left and 26 to 34 percent from the top (just southeast of Korea across a narrow strait), Shikoku and Honshu curving up and to the right toward the top-right corner (Honshu reaching about 84 percent from the left at the top edge).
- A chain of tiny islands (Ryukyu) trails southwest from Kyushu toward Taiwan.
- The wide CENTER and LOWER-RIGHT of the picture is open sea: the East China Sea in the middle between China, Korea and Kyushu, the South China Sea at the bottom center, and the open Pacific Ocean on the right and bottom-right.

Art style: a contemporary Korean picture-book illustration of an old map, hand-painted with opaque gouache and finished with colored-pencil hatching on warm hanji paper with visible paper grain and fibers. The sea is layered muted jade green and soft teal, darker deep indigo toward the open ocean, decorated with small stylized curling wave patterns like Korean folk painting, gentle white foam lines along the coasts. The land is warm ochre and sand beige with tiny painted pine trees, small rounded mountains and soft persimmon-orange accents. A few tiny simple sailing boats and a couple of soft clouds float on the sea as decoration. A decorative compass rose made only of shapes (no letters) sits in the bottom-right ocean. The paper edges are slightly darkened and aged, with a soft warm vignette. Calm, warm, inviting storybook mood. Wide landscape composition, flat top-down map view, not a 3D globe, not photorealistic, not a satellite image.
