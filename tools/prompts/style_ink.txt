Wide painting of one scene from a Korean classical novel (Choe Cheok-jeon), for a story learning game.

Scene: "A Night at the Annam Harbor". A 17th-century international trading port in Annam (Vietnam), like old Hoi An, at night. Many ships of different nations ride at anchor on a calm sea: tall Chinese junks with ribbed batten sails, a large Japanese red-seal trading ship (shuinsen) with a high stern castle and a square mat sail, and small Southeast Asian wooden boats with curved prows. A full moon hangs low over the water and its reflection shimmers on the sea; soft mist drifts between the hulls; a few warm lanterns glow on the decks and along a distant wooden pier with tiled-roof warehouses.
On the deck of the Japanese ship, in the left half of the picture, stands a slender young Korean woman disguised as a man, in plain Joseon commoner mens clothing (a simple undyed white-grey jeogori jacket and baji trousers, hair tied up under a plain cloth headband), listening intently, one hand pressed to her chest, her face turned toward the junk across the water.
On the deck of a Chinese junk across the water, in the right half of the picture, sits a Korean man in his thirties wearing Ming-dynasty Chinese clothing (a long dark blue robe and a simple black cloth cap), playing a long vertical bamboo flute (tungso) toward the moon.
The sound of the flute is shown only subtly, as faint concentric ripples spreading on the moonlit water between the two ships. Quiet, longing, peaceful mood. No fighting, no weapons.
Wide landscape composition, both figures clearly readable.

Art style: a genuine traditional Korean ink-wash painting with light color (sumuk damchae) on hanji paper, in the manner of Joseon literati painters such as Jeong Seon and Kim Hong-do: NOT a detailed illustration. Loose, confident black ink brushstrokes of varied wetness; the ships are only suggested by a few dark strokes for hulls, masts and sails, distant ships fade into pale grey ink; the two figures are drawn with a few simple brush lines, small in the scene but recognizable by pose. Only very light transparent washes of pale indigo, ochre and a touch of vermilion on the lanterns. The moon is left as untouched paper. Mist is soft bleeding wet ink and blank paper. At least half of the picture is empty paper and mist (yeobaek), especially the sky and the water between the two ships. Visible hanji paper fibers. Poetic, airy, quiet and sparse. Absolutely no text, no letters, no characters, no calligraphy, no poem inscription, no inscriptions on flags or sails, no seals, no red seal stamps, no stamps, no signatures, no captions, no watermarks, no frames or borders.
